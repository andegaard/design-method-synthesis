Here are some tips as you create tasks within this band



Failures should spread out

Good low-band tasks show failures at different distances from the gate, by different wrong routes, matching the ablation ladder.
Identical failures across every trial — the same value, the same rung, the same missing correction — mean zero variance and therefore no gradient.
A benchmark item has to separate a better attempt from a worse one; unanimous failure measures nothing.
The crux must be discoverable from the shipped data

This is the biggest single difference between accepted 1/8s and rejected 0/8s.
Ask whether a careful practitioner with only the shipped archive could reach the insight. If not, it's an oracle-knowledge task, not a hard one.
A convention only the author knows is not a judgment call — make it derivable from the data or state it.
Withholding the presence of an effect is usually too much; withholding its size, direction or form is usually right.
Several coupled decisions, not one keystone bit

When one binary choice carries the whole reward, the task is a coin flip regardless of which end of the band it lands on.
Two or more dependent decisions give the score somewhere to live between right and wrong.
If a hedged or non-committal submission can clear the deciding gate, the gate isn't measuring the science.
Gates outside the noise of correct methods

Thresholds should sit well clear of the spread of defensible routes — several times the method noise, not inside it.
Calibrate on 30 reseeds, not one draw. A reference that fails its own reseeds means the gate is fitted to a single realisation.
A genuinely independent implementation is the strongest evidence a task can carry — one that shares the reference's engine, bounds or generator form proves nothing.
Watch for thresholds that penalise standard practice, or that a prior midpoint clears without doing any work.
